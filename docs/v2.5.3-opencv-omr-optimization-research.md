# v2.5.3 experimental optimization — sources, hypothesis, measurement

## Baseline / constraints
Original v2.5.2 Experimental 3 is preserved on `main`. This experiment lives on `experiment/v2.5.3-accuracy-speed`. **Do not merge without real-photo parity and repeated benchmarks.**

We have observed on `Final test 1.jpeg` (same SHA `ab563654a3524fe7b201d594ca63b5f6b3374798e0100e7fde05a241061dd85c`) that 4-marker ArUco rescue verifies 50/50 timing; corner/adaptive is often a failed registration requiring costly QR probing.

## Research and implementation

1. **OMR error modes / geometrical alignment**: `An Enhanced Fault Tolerance Algorithm for Optical Mark Recognition Using Smartphone Cameras`, IEEE Access (2024), DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3451972. Relevant principle: camera pose/skew needs geometric reference verification; preserve QR identity, all timing tracks, and conservative fills. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3451972
2. **Image-ROI / QR detector cost**: `Performance of QR Code Detectors near Nyquist Limits`, Sensors (2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9572759/. Detection+decoding is expensive at limited image resolution; avoid searching entire sheet when expected QR coordinates already known after rectification. Existing regional jsQR decoder remains unchanged; now we render *only* the known 25.25mm ROI instead of rendering a whole A4 before verification.
3. **OpenCV.js/WASM**: official OpenCV build guidance documents WASM SIMD options, https://docs.opencv.org/4.2.0/d4/da1/tutorial_js_setup.html. We do **not** switch binaries or claim SIMD gains: such a change requires reproducible browser benchmarks and compatibility testing.
4. **OMR review**: `Optical Mark Recognition Techniques for Multiple-Choice Tests: A Comprehensive Review`, IEEE (2025), https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11129039 . Real-world noise, ambiguous markings, and misalignment justify conservative UPSC-style fill policy and human-ground-truth tests. We do not change grading thresholds or give experimental AI auto-grade permission.
5. **Classic mark detection**: `A new method of mark detection for software-based optical mark recognition`, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6226159/ , describes pixel-count and template-based methods plus print noise. Keep optical detection; do not introduce uncalibrated decision overrides.

## Exactly what changed
- Four-corner, midpoint, ArUco, hybrid, QR/ArUco registration methods use optional `serializePreview=false` while an alignment is unverified. The baseline toggle restores old serializations.
- A cropped cv.Mat ROI corresponding exactly to the existing decoded-QR region is sent to the SAME jsQR regional routine. Opposite-corner 180° rotation and rescue routines remain active. Successful candidates still produce the full page for overlays and bubble reading.
- Same-image two-pass comparison alternates processing order between v3 baseline (fastQrRoi=false) and v2.5.3 ROI (fastQrRoi=true), **all experimental AI off for both**.
- Candidate traces expose separately measured warp, QR and preview time.

## Required human-ground-truth benchmark
1. Use identical original JPEG in both A/B passes. Run two comparisons on the same page (order alternates); record all runtimes and stage logs.
2. Confirm QR raw identity, Test ID, registration, 50/50 timing and EVERY answer/status are identical before counting speed changes as beneficial. Do not accept a faster but different classification without source-image verification.
3. Repeat `Final test 1.jpeg` and `v5.jpeg`, then at least five distinct photos including rotated, tilted, shadowed and partial marks with a manually labeled answer map.
4. Track median/p90 times separately for cold/warm browser; do not claim speed improvement from a single pair.
5. If any old-valid photo becomes QR-invalid or answer-different, reject or revert this optimization.
